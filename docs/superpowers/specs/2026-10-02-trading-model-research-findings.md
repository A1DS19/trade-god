# Fees close the fast lane; daily models remain

Neither seconds-scale trading nor a trained model trading Bitcoin at horizons of an hour or less is a viable path for a solo trader with under $1,000, and the obstacle is arithmetic and access, not skill or model choice. At Binance's base tier the 2 bp maker fee is about **170 times the BTC perpetual's entire bid-ask spread**, and the average 5-minute BTC move (**9.1 bp**) is smaller than one taker round trip (**10 bp**) (computed from this repo's warehouse, 2026-10-02), while the rebates that make market making pay begin around **$40–110 million of volume a day** ([Hyperliquid fees](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/fees); [Binance LPP](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/binance-updates-usd%E2%93%A2-margined-futures-liquidity-provider-program-2024-06-03-fefc6aa25e0947e2bf745c1c56bea13e)). Trained models call 1–60-minute direction correctly **51–56% of the time** ([Jaquart, Dann & Weinhardt](https://d-nb.info/1276240287/34)) where a taker needs 66% at one hour, and the one study run on the same hourly Binance BTC perpetual data this repo holds turned gross Sharpe ratios of 1.3–2.6 into net losses, with even its cost-aware variants failing to beat buy-and-hold significantly ([Bysik & Ślepaczuk](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00060)). The individuals who did capture Bitcoin's return mostly owned it cheaply and sat still: BTC compounded **34.8% a year from September 2019 through a −76.7% drawdown**, slow trend rules bought shallower drawdowns at the price of return, carry has shrunk to about 3% a year, and BTC beat roughly **90% of surviving altcoins** in every window starting 2022–2025 (computed from this repo's warehouse, 2026-10-02). Daily-to-weekly cross-sectional signals are the one place where peer-reviewed results survive costs, but they are small, decaying and concentrated in illiquid coins, and no audited individual track record was found. For this repo, a daily-horizon model lab is defensible as a measurement instrument rather than an income source, because at under $1,000 even a real edge is worth tens of dollars a year, and the discipline CoinPicks was built around (frozen versions, records committed before their outcomes exist, forward-only validation) is exactly the pre-registration the overfitting literature says such a lab needs.

## Bitcoin paid holders 35% a year for sitting through −77%

The realistic way an individual holds Bitcoin is through an exchange app or, since January 2024, a spot ETF, and the record they buy into rewards only those who sit through it. In this repo's own perpetual closes, BTC went from **$10,307 to $84,830 between September 2019 and October 2026: a 34.8% compound annual return at 61.4% annualised volatility, with a −76.7% drawdown** that bottomed on 21 November 2022. It now sits 31.9% below its $124,628 closing high of 6 October 2025, and both 2025 (−7.4%) and 2026 to date (−4.5%) are down years (computed from this repo's warehouse, 2026-10-02). The start date decides the answer: chain-linked annual returns give about 89% a year from 2013, 58% from 2017, 44% from 2020 and only **13% a year from January 2022** ([Slickcharts](https://www.slickcharts.com/currency/BTC/returns)). Every cycle through 2022 contained a 77–93% drawdown ([Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P164_An_investors_guide.pdf)), regaining the prior high took 20–37 months ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/bitcoin-recover-crash-heres-history-135000264.html)), and the 2025–26 fall of about 52–54% is shallower but unrecovered ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/bitcoin-drawdowns-shrink-recovery-speeds-up-in-2026-cycle)). Calm is no protection: one-year realised volatility set 17 record lows in January 2026, and BTC was below $70,000 by February ([Fidelity](https://institutional.fidelity.com/advisors/insights/topics/investing-ideas/is-bitcoins-four-year-cycle-over)) on its way to about $58,000 in June ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/bitcoin-falls-to-2026-low-amid-higher-us-inflation-data)). Ether, the other long-history major, beat Bitcoin from 2017 but has lost about 7.5% a year since 2022 ([StatMuse](https://www.statmuse.com/money/ask/eth-yearly-returns)) and fell roughly 70% from its high in 2026 ([Techi](https://www.techi.com/ether-price-first-half-2026/)); a 2.6% staking yield ([beaconcha.in](https://beaconcha.in/ethstore)) does not close that gap.

For a small account the route matters more than the timing, because costs and custody risks differ by an order of magnitude between routes.

| Route | Cost | Risk the holder carries |
|---|---|---|
| Spot ETF (IBIT, FBTC, Grayscale Mini) | 0.15–0.25% a year | Broker and fund; no on-chain use |
| Legacy trusts (GBTC, ETHE) | 1.5–2.5% a year, ≈14–22% of wealth over ten years | Same, plus the fee drag |
| App "simple buy" (Coinbase) | ≈1.49% fee plus ≈0.5% spread per purchase | Exchange custody and failure |
| Exchange order book (Coinbase Advanced, lowest tier) | 0.60% maker, 1.20% taker | Exchange custody and failure |
| Self-custody wallet | Network fees | Key loss, theft, physical coercion |

The ETF fees come from [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/investing/learn/spot-bitcoin-etf) and [CryptoPotato](https://cryptopotato.com/best-ethereum-etfs/), the Coinbase costs from [Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/coinbase-fees), and the ten-year drag is compounded from the fee rates. Custody is where holders have lost money outside the market itself. FTX fixed claims at the petition-date price of $16,871, which left BTC creditors with about 20% of market value in bitcoin terms by May 2025 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/348774/ftx-major-repayments-to-begin-may-30-btc-claims-currently-worth-just-20-of-market-price)); Mt. Gox creditors from 2014 are still being repaid under a deadline extended to 31 October 2026 ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/346053/mt-gox-pushes-back-bitcoin-repayments-by-another-year)); more than $3.4 billion was stolen in 2025, Bybit's $1.5 billion included ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/crypto-hacking-stolen-funds-2026/)); and self-custodians faced 95 attempted physical "wrench" attacks in 2025 alone ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/violent-crypto-wrench-attacks-2026/)).

How to buy matters less than how much. Vanguard's 1976–2022 study found a lump sum beat three-month cost averaging **68% of the time**, with averaging ahead mainly in the worst quarter of outcomes ([Vanguard](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/cost_averaging_invest_now_or_temporarily_hold_your_cash.pdf)); a broker-authored bitcoin study found lump sums accumulated more BTC on average, with dollar-cost averaging ahead only in bear stretches such as 2021–23 ([Swan](https://nakamotoportfolio.com/static/docs/DCA_Lumpsum.pdf)). No peer-reviewed crypto study of the question exists, and for a sub-$1,000 buyer the ≈2% cost of each app purchase can exceed the whole difference between the two methods, so the cheap route comes first. On size, BlackRock calls 1–2% of a 60/40 portfolio reasonable, noting that 1% of BTC adds about 2% of portfolio risk and 4% adds about 14% ([BlackRock Investment Institute](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/blackrock-investment-institute-says-investors-202745363.html)), and Morgan Stanley's range is 0–4% ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2025/10/07/morgan-stanley-recommends-a-4-opportunistic-crypto-portfolio-allocation)). A small, rebalanced sleeve is the best-documented way to hold the asset: 2.5% BTC rebalanced quarterly lifted a 60/40 portfolio's Sharpe ratio from 0.27 to 0.54 over 2014–2020 without deepening its drawdown, although Bitwise concedes its best result, from yearly rebalancing, owed much to lucky dates ([Bitwise](https://static.bitwiseinvestments.com/Research/Bitwise-The-Case-For-Crypto-In-An-Institutional-Portfolio.pdf)). The diversification fails when it is wanted: BTC's correlation with equities rose from 0.04 in normal quarters to 0.71 when stocks fell more than 20% ([Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P164_An_investors_guide.pdf)).

What individuals actually earn trails the asset, because they buy after rallies. Rising prices pull new users in, and the BIS estimates that **73–81% of retail investors likely lost money** on their first bitcoin purchases across 95 countries in 2015–22 ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1049.htm)); after Terra and FTX, large holders sold while small ones bought ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull69.htm)). The 200,000 traders in one eToro sample were contrarian in stocks but chased momentum in crypto ([Kogan et al.](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31317)). IBIT returned about 46% a year from January 2024 to November 2025 while its investors' dollar-weighted return was about 11% ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com.au/etfs/bitcoin-etf-doubled-value-its-investors-made-only-one-fourth-that)), though part of that gap is the fund growing from zero during its best months, the distortion a 2026 Financial Analysts Journal paper says inflates such measures ([CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2026/bad-timing-does-not-cost-investors-funds-returns)). Taxes then punish turnover harder than fees do: US short-term gains face rates up to 37% against 20% long-term ([TokenTax](https://tokentax.co/blog/bitcoin-etfs-tax)), and Germany exempts gains on coins held over a year until a proposed 2027 change ([Koinly](https://koinly.io/guides/crypto-tax-germany/); [Market Periodical](https://themarketperiodical.com/2026/09/10/germany-eyes-25-crypto-gains-tax-from-2027-as-holding-exemption-is-scrapped/)). At top US rates, a strategy earning 30% a year whose gains are all realised short-term compounds at about 19% after tax, against about 27% for the same return held and sold in year ten (computed illustration, ignoring losses and state tax).

## Slow rules cut drawdowns, carry shrank, and 90% of altcoins trailed Bitcoin

Trend following is the best-documented way to trade Bitcoin at low turnover, and what it buys is a smaller drawdown, not a bigger return. Scaled to 10% volatility and before costs, BTC momentum rules over 22 to 261 days earned Sharpe ratios of 1.46–1.65 against 1.18 for buy-and-hold over 2016–2022 ([Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P164_An_investors_guide.pdf)). A study of long-only breakout rules on a survivorship-free coin set reported an ensemble CAGR of 30%, volatility of 17% and a **19% maximum drawdown against more than 80% for passive BTC** over 2015–2025, before costs and with up to 2× leverage from its volatility target; its 360-day rule traded five times in a decade, while its 5-day rule traded 292 times and nearly halved its CAGR at 50 bp a trade ([Zarattini, Pagani & Barbon](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Catching-Crypto-Trends.pdf)). Buy-and-hold compounded about 76% a year over 2015–2024 (computed from [Slickcharts](https://www.slickcharts.com/currency/BTC/returns) annual returns), so trend's advantage was risk-adjusted, earned by sitting out 2014–15, 2018 and 2022. Out-of-sample support is thinner: a 15,000-rule test found **"no predictability for Bitcoin in the out-of-sample period"** ([Hudson & Urquhart](https://d-nb.info/1202710646/34)), and a 2026 study on Binance data found that slow signals beat fast ones, which overreact to noise ([Kang & Ryu](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41283-026-00234-7)).

This repo's data shows the recent regime has been less kind. Unoptimised textbook rules on BTC perpetual closes, charged 10 bp per unit of turnover, produced these in-sample results:

| Rule, 2020-03-27 → 2026-10-01 | CAGR | Max drawdown | Volatility | CAGR ÷ max drawdown |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buy and hold | 48.9% | −76.7% | 57.4% | 0.64 |
| 200-day SMA filter, long or cash | 40.9% | −64.1% | 42.9% | 0.64 |
| Volatility target, 40% a year, capped at 1× | 36.4% | −64.2% | 40.9% | 0.57 |

_Source: computed from this repo's warehouse, 2026-10-02._

Neither rule improved return per unit of drawdown; both bought lower volatility with return. The trend filter's worst stretch shows why. In the May 2021 crash its equity fell from 1.028 to 0.579 before BTC crossed below its 200-day average, and 19 whipsaw switches then carried it down to 0.412 by December (computed from this repo's warehouse, 2026-10-02). Slow rules dodge long bears, not sharp crashes, and if bears stay as shallow as 2026's ≈−54% rather than the historical 77–93%, the main source of trend following's historical advantage shrinks with them. Volatility targeting helps only at the margin: it raised BTC's Sharpe ratio from 1.08 to 1.20 in one sample ([Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P164_An_investors_guide.pdf)), but with crypto's power-law tails "risk management does not change the tail risk" ([Grobys et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)).

Delta-neutral cash-and-carry (long spot, short the perpetual, collect the funding) was crypto's best risk-adjusted trade and has mostly closed. Summed 8-hourly BTC funding on Binance paid **30.6% in 2021, 12.0% in 2024, 5.1% in 2025 and 2.2% through 1 October 2026, about 3% annualised**, with a quarter of 2026's funding periods negative (computed from this repo's warehouse, 2026-10-02). Borri et al. measure the perpetual carry trade's Sharpe ratio falling from 6.45 over 2020–2025 to 4.06 since 2024 and **below zero in 2025** ([Borri, Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)), and CME's annualised basis of 5.7–7.9% in August 2026 barely cleared a 4.19% two-year Treasury ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/bitcoin-futures-carry-treasury-yield-etf-flows/)). Those Sharpe ratios also hide how carry dies. Galois Capital lost about $40 million, half its assets, on FTX ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2023/02/20/crypto-hedge-fund-galois-shuts-down-after-losing-40-million-to-ftx-ft)), and on 10 October 2025 auto-deleveraging closed profitable hedge legs while USDe collateral priced at about $0.66 on Binance alone ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/october-10-crypto-crash-explained)). At today's ≈3%, a $1,000 carry book earns about $30 a year before fees on two legs, less than Treasuries pay. This repo's June 2026 research rejected *directional* carry; the hedged version was never tested, and at this account size it would not pay.

Altcoins face a harsher base rate. Among the 219 symbols in the warehouse, all still listed and therefore flattering to altcoins, the record against BTC for windows ending 1 October 2026 was:

| Window start | BTC | Altcoins alive all window | Beat BTC | Median altcoin | Lost more than 50% | ETH |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-01-01 | +189% | 26 | 10 (38%) | +77% | 15% | +271% |
| 2022-01-01 | +78% | 37 | 3 (8%) | −82% | 65% | −28% |
| 2023-01-01 | +411% | 44 | 4 (9%) | −11% | 34% | +125% |
| 2024-01-01 | +92% | 59 | 6 (10%) | −61% | 54% | +15% |
| 2025-01-01 | −10% | 81 | 7 (9%) | −73% | 75% | −19% |

_Source: computed from this repo's warehouse, 2026-10-02. Survivors only._

Only the window that opened inside the 2021 mania gave altcoins a fair fight. Every window opening in 2022–2025 saw **BTC beat about 90% of surviving altcoins**, with the median one down 61–82% in three of the four. External snapshots agree: 9 of October 2025's top 50 altcoins beat BTC from its peak to late September 2026 ([CryptoPotato](https://cryptopotato.com/only-9-of-the-top-50-altcoins-have-beaten-bitcoin-since-its-peak-picking-the-winners-is-brutal/)), and only 10 of 2017's top 50 (BTC, ETH, USDT, XRP, DOGE, LTC, XLM, XMR, ETC and ZEC) were still in the top 50 five years later ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/money/cryptocurrency/articles/youll-be-shocked-to-learn-which-10-cryptos-are-still-in-the-top-50-after-five-years)). "Long-history coins" is a survivor list by construction, and survivorship plus delisting bias inflates equal-weighted crypto portfolio returns by **about 62% a year** ([Ammann et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4287573)).

Cross-sectional strategies, which rank coins against one another, show large gross premia that thin under realism. Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu found weekly long-short momentum returns of 2.3–4.5%, an effect concentrated in large coins, with long-only 3-week momentum in the top 20 still earning 4.22% a week after fees and bid-ask costs ([Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13119)), and Borri et al. find 2-week momentum still significant after 2020 at 2.1% a week ([Borri et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)). Yet plain momentum among the top 30 coins turned negative from August 2020 to December 2023 ([Grobys et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)), and a test with costs and intraday liquidation risk found cross-sectional momentum "almost non-existent" ([Han, Kang & Ryu](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4675565)); the conflict over post-2020 momentum is unresolved. This repo met the small-account version of the problem directly. Its June 2026 cross-sectional momentum test reached a pooled out-of-sample profit factor of 1.034, none of 54 combinations reached the pre-registered 1.2, and at $25–50 a position the book would have netted about $33–50 a year against about $93 of fees ([Phase C design](2026-06-12-quant-strategy-overhaul-design.md); computed from this repo's warehouse, 2026-10-02).

## Seconds-scale trading is a rebate business closed to a $1,000 account

Real crypto high-frequency trading is market making with fee rebates and private gateways, arbitrage and hedging across venues, and seconds-scale taking on order-flow signals, run by a concentrated set of firms with access a retail account cannot buy. Binance's low-latency futures endpoints are "available only to eligible high-frequency trading users who have enrolled and qualified in at least one of the Futures Liquidity Provider Programs" ([Binance](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/what-are-binance-futures-low-latency-api-services-7df7f3838c3b49e692d175374c3a3283)). The CFTC's complaint quotes Binance describing one firm as "a top 5 client and 12% of our volume" and documents dedicated gateways worth a "5-10ms latency reduction on roundtrip" ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/media/8351/%20enfbinancecomplaint032723/download)). On Hyperliquid's builder-deployed markets, 8 market-maker wallets did half of all market-maker volume ([Arrakis](https://arrakis.finance/blog/who-is-trading-on-hip3)), and the flow a slow quote meets is persistently informed: the most toxic 5% of taker wallets earn a 10-second markout of 3.11 bp, and a wallet's toxicity carries over from one 10-day window to the next ([Zhai](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04373v3)).

Latency sorts traders into three tiers. Firms inside an exchange's AWS placement group see host-to-host round trips of about 18–24 microseconds ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/web3/optimize-tick-to-trade-latency-for-digital-assets-exchanges-and-trading-platforms-on-aws-part-2/)). Anyone can rent a Tokyo server within 6–8 ms of Hyperliquid and Binance ([CCXT](https://docs.ccxt.com/blog/how-far-is-your-exchange)), where OKX moved its latency-sensitive services in July and Bybit moves on 12 November ([OKX](https://www.okx.com/en-us/help/okx-us-trading-server-migration-announcement-hong-kong-tokyo); [Bybit](https://announcements.bybitglobal.com/en/article/bybit-trading-server-migration-from-aws-singapore-to-aws-tokyo--art853451a43a75/)). A home connection in Europe or the Americas pays a "100–350 ms tax" ([CCXT](https://docs.ccxt.com/blog/how-far-is-your-exchange)), which on Hyperliquid is a 200 ms disadvantage ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/03/30/hyperliquid-traders-in-tokyo-get-200-millisecond-edge-glassnode-research-shows)). Hardware is not the barrier, since a machine matching Hyperliquid's recommended node spec lists at about $1.61 an hour ([Vantage](https://instances.vantage.sh/aws/ec2/m7i.8xlarge?region=ap-northeast-1)); access is, in the form of gateways, message-limit exemptions, placement groups and queue priority. One practitioner replaying a maker strategy from a European laptop had **24% of post-only orders rejected** because the market moved while they were in flight ([HFTENGINE](https://github.com/mirkovicdev/HFTENGINE)).

Fees then swamp the moves. Base-tier BTC perpetual fees are about 2 bp maker and 4.5–5.5 bp taker on every major venue, so a taker round trip costs 9–11 bp and a maker round trip 3–4 bp ([BitDegree](https://www.bitdegree.org/crypto/tutorials/binance-fees); [Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/bybit-vip-levels-explained); [OKX](https://www.okx.com/en-us/help/updates-to-global-fee-framework); [Hyperliquid](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/fees)). On this repo's BTC perpetual bars, the move a trade must beat falls below the fee at short horizons:

| Horizon | Mean move | Return autocorrelation | Absolute-return autocorrelation | Break-even hit rate, taker (10 bp) | Break-even hit rate, maker (4 bp) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 minutes | 9.1 bp | −0.013 | 0.341 | none possible | 72.0% |
| 15 minutes | 15.8 bp | −0.009 | 0.316 | 81.6% | 62.7% |
| 1 hour | 31.3 bp | −0.010 | 0.268 | 66.0% | 56.4% |
| 4 hours | 63.6 bp | +0.017 | 0.162 | 57.9% | 53.1% |
| 1 day | 163.3 bp | −0.074 | 0.198 | 53.1% | 51.2% |

_Source: computed from this repo's warehouse, 2026-10-02, on BTCUSDT perpetual bars from 2025-01-14 to 2026-07-15, with lag-1 autocorrelations. Break-even is 0.5 + cost ÷ (2 × mean move), which assumes symmetric payoffs; the maker column ignores adverse selection and so flatters makers._

Direction is unpredictable from its own past at every horizon while volatility clusters, and at 5 minutes **the average move is smaller than one taker round trip**, so no hit rate breaks even. The predictable pieces the literature has found are fractions of a fee. At one second, wallet-identity features lift out-of-sample R² on Hyperliquid's busiest perpetuals to about 12% ([Zhai](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04373v3)), which on a one-second BTC volatility of about 0.75 bp, scaled from a 42% annual figure ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/data/crypto-markets/prices/annualized-btc-volatility-30d)), is worth roughly 0.25 bp (computed). The quarter-hour burst on Binance perpetuals is worth "about 0.5 bp per boundary, about one tenth of a single standard-tier taker fee" ([Kim & Hansen](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09426v2)), and 15-minute mean reversion appears in 90% of 183 Binance pairs yet peaks near 1.3 bp gross, so that **"not one of the 183 crypto pairs clears even the 5 bp maker band"** ([Kitron & Wengrowicz](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21888)).

Posting limit orders does not escape the arithmetic. On 2 October 2026 the BTC perpetual's spread was $0.10 on an $84,604 price, **0.0118 bp, so the 2 bp base maker fee was about 170 times the entire spread** (computed from this repo's warehouse, 2026-10-02). Resting orders are also adversely selected: live top-of-book maker orders on Binance's BTC perpetual averaged about −0.8 bp at one second, and naive two-sided quoting "lost almost 60%" in about three days with latency assumed to be zero ([Albers et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18625v2)). For directional signals worked with limit orders, fills came only 8–25% of the time, and the trades that filled earned −4.3 bp while those that missed would have earned +4.9 bp ([The Microstructure Lab](https://themicrostructurelab.substack.com/p/execution-simulation-for-hf-directional)). Market making pays only inside rebate programs, which begin at 0.20% of Binance's weekly maker volume, about $108 million a day at August 2026 volumes ([Binance](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/binance-updates-usd%E2%93%A2-margined-futures-liquidity-provider-program-2024-06-03-fefc6aa25e0947e2bf745c1c56bea13e); [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/crypto-spot-derivatives-volume-august-2026/)), or 0.5% of Hyperliquid's 14-day maker volume, about $40 million a day ([Hyperliquid](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/fees)). For a $1,000 account that is **40,000 times its capital every day**. Even Hyperliquid's message rules bind: an address earns one request per USDC it trades ([Hyperliquid](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/rate-limits-and-user-limits)), so refreshing a single quote once a second needs about $86,000 of fills a day, and at the 1.5 bp base maker fee those fills cost about $13 a day, 1.3% of the account, to chase a half-spread of about 0.06 bp on BTC's $1 tick ([Hyperliquid](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/tick-and-lot-size)) (computed). The strongest counterpoints leave the picture intact. Binance's March 2026 cuts lowered VIP 3 to $50 million of monthly futures volume ([Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/binance-slashes-vip-entry-thresholds-to-support-global-growth/)), still 50,000 times a $1,000 account each month, and the one study reporting positive annual returns on 3-second signals (0.07–7.00%) does not model latency and calls itself an upper bound ([Bieganowski & Ślepaczuk](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00776v1)). The passive way into the business, depositing in Hyperliquid's HLP vault, recently yielded about 4% a year ([Vaultvision](https://vaultvision.tech/vaults/drawdown)) with multi-million-dollar losses on thin listings such as POPCAT and JELLYJELLY along the way ([Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/hyperliquid-hlp-explained)).

Population evidence says the same about people. Of Brazilians who day traded index futures for more than 300 days, **97% lost money** and 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage ([Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)). In Taiwan fewer than 1% of day traders predictably profit after fees ([Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=529063)), and losers keep trading almost as often as winners ([Barber et al.](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/Day%20Trading%20and%20Learning%20110217.pdf)). Regulators count 74–89% of EU retail CFD accounts ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/library/esma71-98-128_press_release_product_intervention.pdf)) and 68% of Australian ones losing money ([ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-004mr-asic-secures-nearly-40-million-in-refunds-to-investors-and-drives-change-after-cfd-sector-falls-short/)). Crypto adds leverage, with liquidated BitMEX traders averaging about 60× ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3781646)), and a third-party study of 10,000 Hyperliquid wallets, with opaque sampling, found 16.5% profitable, rising from **15.3% of $0–100 accounts to 85.7% of $10–100 million ones** ([Medium](https://medium.com/@envyprotocol/i-analyzed-10-000-hyperliquid-traders-the-results-are-brutal-a29adcca8c2a)). Retail automation fares no better in theory, since a classic grid bot's expected return is "essentially zero" under standard assumptions ([Chen, Chen & Jang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11921)). The viral counterexample, a Hyperliquid rebate bot said to have turned $6,800 into $1.5 million in two weeks, fails its own arithmetic: $1.4 billion of volume at a 0.003% rebate is $42,000, not the $420,000 reported ([TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/28173)). Professionals lose on-venue too: four labelled market makers lost $23.5 million on Hyperliquid in 30 days ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/four-market-makers-on-hyperliquid-lose-over-23-5m-in-30-days)) on positions that hedge books held elsewhere, a business a single-venue trader cannot copy.

Finally, this repo could not study the question with what it holds. The warehouse has no order-book or tick data; Binance's free tick-level top-of-book archive ends around March 2024, Tardis's free monthly sample files return 404, and only trade-level and depth-snapshot files are current (computed from this repo's warehouse, 2026-10-02). A seconds-scale lab would begin with months of building a Tokyo-hosted recorder, to answer a question the evidence has already settled.

## Models call direction at 51–56% where fees demand 66% or more

Trained models do find structure in short-horizon Bitcoin prices; it is smaller than the fee. Jaquart, Dann and Weinhardt's recurrent networks, tree ensembles and logistic models reached **50.9–56.0% directional accuracy** at 1–60 minutes, and their best long-short rule made 116% gross in a three-month test, but at a 30 bp round trip "trading performance becomes negative for all methods" ([Jaquart, Dann & Weinhardt](https://d-nb.info/1276240287/34)). Read against the warehouse table (illustratively, since the periods differ), even their best 60-minute model (56.0%) sits just under the optimistic maker break-even at one hour (56.4%), a figure that already ignores adverse selection. The closest study to this repo's data used Binance's hourly BTCUSDT perpetual from 2018 to 2026 with 27 rolling walk-forward folds, and its XGBoost, LSTM and iTransformer models turned gross Sharpe ratios of **1.27–2.59 into net Sharpe ratios of −1.16 to −1.82** at 10 bp per position change, over 8,448–17,942 trades. Trading only when the forecast exceeded twice the cost cut the count to 69–251 trades and lifted net Sharpe to 0.64–1.09, yet **"none of the reported cost-aware strategies significantly outperforms buy-and-hold"**; the authors locate the obstacle in "not only weak predictability, but also the way forecasts are converted into trades" ([Bysik & Ślepaczuk](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00060)).

Order-book deep learning posts the headline accuracies: 78% on next-tick BTC mid-price direction ([Fang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00803)), 71% at two seconds ([Jha et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01241)), and 74.7% F1 at 10 events on Binance's BTC perpetual, falling to 41.5% at 100 events ([Berti & Kasneci](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15757v3)). All of it is on moves smaller than a fee, with no costed profit and loss, and a benchmark of 15 such models found that "all models exhibit a significant performance drop when exposed to new data" ([Prata et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01915)). Briola, Bartolucci and Aste warn that "achieving high scores on these traditional machine learning metrics does not necessarily translate into the generation of actionable trading signals" ([Briola, Bartolucci & Aste](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12315853/)). Public competitions give the honest scale of minute-level signal. G-Research's crypto contest was won by plain LightGBM models whose feature engineering "had a far greater impact on their final scores than model development" ([G-Research](https://www.gresearch.com/news/wrapping-up-the-g-research-crypto-forecasting-competition/)); a leakage-aware individual replication reached an information coefficient of 0.021 ([GitHub](https://github.com/Jessiii-mo/G-Research-Crypto)); and DRW's 2025 winner reportedly reached 0.131 with about 890 proprietary features no outsider has ([GitHub](https://github.com/coderback/drw-crypto-market-prediction)). At an information coefficient of 0.05 and 50% annual volatility, a one-standard-deviation signal is worth about 0.8 bp on a 5-minute bar, 2.7 bp on an hourly bar and 13 bp on a daily bar against a 10 bp round trip (computed); only the daily bar clears it.

Daily-to-weekly cross-sectional prediction is the one family with peer-reviewed after-cost results. CTREND, a machine-learning aggregate of 28 technical signals over more than 3,000 coins, "survives the impact of transaction costs and persists in big and liquid coins" for holding periods up to four weeks ([Fieberg et al.](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/trend-factor-for-the-cross-section-of-cryptocurrency-returns/4C1509ACBA33D5DCAF0AC24379148178)), though its sample ends in May 2022. Cakici et al. find that most machine-learning strategies stay profitable after costs, but that "alphas are concentrated in hard-to-trade assets," mostly in the long leg ([Cakici et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4295427)). The striking Sharpe ratio of 3.23 for a daily top-100 classifier assumes mid-price fills, free shorting and no costs ([Jaquart, Köpke & Weinhardt](https://d-nb.info/1276240287/34)). The only post-2022 out-of-sample, after-cost result found, in which LLM agents proposed factors in a point-in-time factor language tested on 2024–2026, fell from **a Sharpe ratio of 1.55 at 5 bp one-way to 0.46 at 30 bp** ([Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.26747v1)), so the cost model largely decides whether a result exists at all. Practitioners keep such books small: Man Group gets the best trend Sharpe from 10–15 coins because liquidity "starts to decline significantly" beyond the top 15 ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/in-crypto-we-trend)).

The newer model families have weaker evidence still:

| Approach | Best available test | What survived costs |
|---|---|---|
| Boosted trees, LSTMs, transformers on 1-minute to 1-hour bars | 2019 minute data; 2018–2026 hourly BTC perpetual | Nothing; cost-aware variants did not beat buy-and-hold |
| Order-book deep learning | Next tick to 2 seconds, 71–78% accuracy | No costed P&L; accuracy decays on new data |
| Daily-to-weekly cross-sectional ML | CTREND, Cakici et al., Huang et al. | Positive, but illiquid-tail, cost-sensitive, mostly pre-2022 |
| LLM agents trading live | Alpha Arena, two-week seasons | Aggregate −29.5% and −35.2% |
| Reinforcement learning | FinRL crypto contest, 45-minute test | +0.66% against +0.74% for holding BTC |
| Time-series foundation models | Daily equity returns in 94 countries; five US stocks | Do not reliably beat a random walk |

In Alpha Arena's first season, six frontier models each traded $10,000 of real money on Hyperliquid for about two weeks; the pool ended at $42,298, from Qwen3 Max's +22.3% to GPT-5's −58.7% ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/)). In Season 1.5 only 6 of 32 accounts finished positive, the group lost 35.2%, and the Season 1 winner lost in all four of its modes, according to a third-party leaderboard compilation of medium reliability ([compilation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/josejuarez96/tradepartner/main/docs/research/2026-09-26-g6-alpha-arena.md)). Two weeks of data leave a Sharpe ratio's standard error near 5, so these rankings are noise. Backtested LLM agents look better only inside their training window: an audit of five published systems found 35 of 40 friction components unmodelled and FinMem's return falling about 72% once the test crossed its model's pre-training cutoff ([The Alpha Illusion](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16895v1)), consistent with evidence that LLMs recall pre-cutoff market values while "post-cutoff, we observe no recall" ([Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14765)). The FinRL contest's crypto result came from a test window of about 45 minutes in which its ensemble returned 0.66% and holding BTC returned 0.74% ([Holzer et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.10709)); reinforcement learning's documented crypto success is execution, where it cut limit-order costs by up to 37% ([Schnaubelt](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221721003854)). Zero-shot Chronos and TimesFM produced negative out-of-sample R² on daily returns, −1.37% and −2.80% ([Rahimikia, Ni & Wang](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18578v1)), and a September 2026 survey of the field concludes that "no general AI architecture is shown to deliver persistent, cross-regime, capacity-aware net alpha" ([Zhu & Cai](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04917)).

Behind every one of these results sits the base rate for backtests. With five years of daily data, no more than about 45 variations of a strategy should be tried before a Sharpe ratio of 1 becomes likely by chance, and an optimised in-sample Sharpe of 1.59 on random data became −0.18 out of sample ([Bailey et al.](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/overfit-tools-at.pdf)). Across 888 Quantopian algorithms, backtest Sharpe explained under 2.5% of the variance in out-of-sample Sharpe ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/)); 215 bank-marketed strategies lost a median **73% of their Sharpe ratio** going live, the most complex decaying most ([Suhonen, Lennkh & Perez](https://www.pm-research.com/content/iijpormgmt/43/2/90)); and published predictors earn 58% less after publication ([McLean & Pontiff](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365)). López de Prado's warning fits a one-person lab exactly: "If you have been asked to develop ML strategies on your own, the odds are stacked against you" ([López de Prado](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3104816)). What is reliably forecastable is risk. Realised-variance HAR models beat GARCH in crypto volatility forecasting ([Bergsli et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/eeeriibaf/v_3a59_3ay_3a2022_3ai_3ac_3as0275531921001616.htm)), and funding-rate levels forecast out of sample ([Inan](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5576424)) while funding's predictive R² for the next period's BTC price change is about zero ([Presto Labs](https://www.prestolabs.io/research/can-funding-rate-predict-price-change)); no peer-reviewed evidence was found that open interest or long/short ratios predict returns. The warehouse's autocorrelations say the same thing: model risk to size positions, not direction to pick them.

## The lab's product is a verdict, built on CoinPicks' rules

### The repo has already measured what the literature measures

This repo's record converges with the external evidence: "Four systems — DCA, swing v1, swing v2, intraday — now all measure negative" ([decisions log](../../../agents/decisions.md)). The last of them, a 15-minute oversold-VWAP bounce that was the sole survivor of six pre-registered families, carried a next-bar gross edge of about 0.083% against a 0.16% round-trip hurdle ([edge-hunt findings](2026-07-15-intraday-edge-hunt-findings.md)) and failed its sealed out-of-sample test at **−15.19%, profit factor 0.986 and Sharpe −0.29 over 2,791 trades** ([out-of-sample results](../../../research/signals/intraday/output/2b/oos_results.csv)), a pattern the findings call "not capturable at retail costs with bar-level execution" ([2b findings](2026-07-15-mr-vwap-2b-findings.md)). About five weeks later, Kitron and Wengrowicz published the same conclusion across 183 Binance pairs. The paper run's +$18.18 over 312 trades became −$0.26 without its ten best trades, which were mostly fresh low-cap listings (computed from this repo's warehouse, 2026-10-02). The short-horizon experiment has been run here once, cheaply (real-money exposure was $8 DCA buys and $5 positions at 5×, plus a $100 paper account), and it found what the literature finds.

### A daily horizon swaps the cost problem for a sample-size problem

The owner's choice of a daily horizon follows the evidence: at one day the taker break-even falls to 53.1%, and a modest information coefficient of 0.05 is worth about 13 bp a bar against a 10 bp round trip. The binding constraint changes rather than disappears. At 5 minutes the warehouse holds 157,535 BTC bars in the common window and the fee exceeds the average move; at one day the fee is 6% of the average move, but BTC has only **2,580 daily bars since September 2019** (computed from this repo's warehouse, 2026-10-02). On Lo's approximation the standard error of an annual Sharpe ratio is about 1.2 after one year of data and 0.6 after four, so seven years of single-asset daily history pins a Sharpe ratio only to within roughly ±0.4–0.5 before any penalty for the trials run (computed), and Bailey's budget for five years of daily data is about 45 trials. Cross-sectional models buy statistical power back by ranking many coins each day, which is also where the published after-cost evidence sits, but that is where survivorship bites, and every one of the warehouse's 219 symbols is still listed. A point-in-time universe that includes delisted perpetuals is therefore the lab's first piece of infrastructure, not a refinement. Two smaller data gaps follow from the same logic. HAR-style volatility models want intraday realised variance, but the 5-minute and 15-minute datasets were left out of the daily refresh and stop on 2026-07-15 (computed from this repo's warehouse, 2026-10-02); and the open-interest and long/short series, which have no peer-reviewed predictive record, carry a permanent hole from June to late August 2026 ([CLAUDE.md](../../../CLAUDE.md)). The opposite move, another hourly BTC direction model, has already been run on this exact data by Bysik and Ślepaczuk, so a "no" from it would teach nothing new.

### CoinPicks' rules become the lab's pre-registration

With CoinPicks' code and Postgres archived under `legacy/coinpicks/`, what carries over is its discipline rather than its tables, and that discipline maps item by item onto what the overfitting literature asks of a credible test. CoinPicks stamped a frozen `SCORING_VERSION` on every score and compared ledger rows "only within one origin and one combination of versions" ([automated-research design](2026-09-22-automated-research-design.md)); a lab needs the same rule, so that results from two models, cost models or universes are never silently pooled. CoinPicks made each report immutable before its outcome existed; the lab's equivalent is the freeze, in which parameters chosen on train are committed before the sealed out-of-sample period is touched and then judged once against gates fixed in advance. This repo already practised that form in Phase 2b, where the plan and code were committed before any real-data run and [`frozen_params.json`](../../../research/signals/intraday/output/2b/frozen_params.json) was fixed before a single unseal: "one grid (18 combos × 2 variants), one freeze, one unseal" ([2b findings](2026-07-15-mr-vwap-2b-findings.md)). CoinPicks' ruling that "a ledger holding only passes is survivorship bias" ([decisions log](../../../agents/decisions.md)) becomes a count of every trial, failures included, which is the input a deflated Sharpe ratio ([Bailey & López de Prado](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/324663771_The_Deflated_Sharpe_Ratio_Correcting_for_Selection_Bias_Backtest_Overfitting_and_Non-Normality)) or a probability of backtest overfitting ([Bailey et al.](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)) needs. Its frozen 30/90/180/365-day horizons become forward evaluation fixed before any outcome exists, and its forward-only stance (web pages cannot be replayed, so a backtest would be look-ahead) carries over intact to any LLM component, because a model that memorised the test period cannot be backtested on it.

If the fundamental scores are ever revived, the evidence says how to judge them: as one hypothesis among the lab's experiments rather than a product to trade, and against BTC rather than against zero. With about 90% of surviving altcoins trailing BTC in every window since 2022, a score that merely avoids the worst coins can look predictive in raw returns and still lose to holding Bitcoin. The nearest thing to a fundamental signal in the factor literature is Borri et al.'s on-chain value proxy, price relative to new addresses, which produced a significant weekly long-short spread of −3.5% (t = −3.50, and −2.3% after 2020) before costs ([Borri et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)): evidence that fundamentals deserve a test, not that they would pass one. The framework's hunting ground, coins with low liquidity for the cycle that mostly sit below the top 100 ([decisions log](../../../agents/decisions.md)), is where published cross-sectional alpha concentrates and where a small account's one structural advantage, the absence of any capacity limit, applies. It is also where survivorship inflates backtests most and spreads are widest, and the warehouse holds no order-book data to measure those spreads.

### The first experiment the evidence supports

| Choice | What the evidence supports | Grounds |
|---|---|---|
| Horizon | Daily decisions, holding one to four weeks | Taker break-even 53.1% at one day; CTREND significant to four weeks |
| Universe | Point-in-time, liquidity-filtered 15–30 Binance perpetuals, delisted ones included | Liquidity falls beyond the top 15; survivorship inflates equal-weighted returns about 62% a year |
| Targets, in order | Volatility for sizing, then cross-sectional rank; single-asset BTC direction last | Absolute-return autocorrelation 0.16–0.34; hourly BTC direction already tested negative |
| Model | Gradient-boosted trees on engineered price, volume and funding features | Competition winners; no net evidence for RL, foundation models or LLM agents |
| Costs | Taker 5 bp per side plus slippage and funding; must also pass the stress variant with slippage doubled | The repo's own cost model and Phase C criteria |
| Validation | Purged, embargoed walk-forward on train; every trial counted; deflated Sharpe; one unseal of a sealed out-of-sample period | About 45 trials per five years of daily data |
| Baselines to beat | BTC buy-and-hold, 200-day SMA filter, volatility target, equal-weight basket | Warehouse CAGR ÷ max drawdown of 0.64, 0.64 and 0.57 |
| Before real money | A forward record of at least 12 months | Sharpe standard error about 1.2 after one year |

Two exclusions follow from the same evidence. Seconds-scale work stays out unless the cost side changes, because fees and access, not forecasting skill, are what bind there; a better model would not change that. LLMs belong upstream, proposing features or factors under point-in-time rules as in the one positive crypto LLM result ([Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.26747v1)) and evaluated only on data after their training cutoff, rather than trading, where the live record is −29.5% and −35.2%. The money at stake should match the evidence too. A model that beat buy-and-hold by a few percentage points a year would add tens of dollars to a $1,000 account, the same order as the ≈$93 fee bill that sank Phase C's momentum book (computed from this repo's warehouse, 2026-10-02), so the lab's return is the verdict, and the owner has already accepted "no edge found" as one. When a forward record eventually justifies real money, a slice at the trading era's scale, a few dollars a position, is enough to test execution without paying for the same lesson twice.

## Conclusion

Laid side by side, the evidence makes "which model?" the least important question. At seconds-scale horizons the constraint is fees and access, which no model touches; at daily horizons it is statistical power and survivorship, which only evaluation infrastructure fixes; and wherever the tests were honest, simple models won, from plain LightGBM taking the top three places in the G-Research contest to simple strategies decaying less than complex ones once live. For a solo lab the scarce input is not compute or architecture but uncontaminated out-of-sample data, and the only unlimited source of it is the future, recorded immutably as it arrives. That makes the discipline this repo built for CoinPicks worth more than the product it was built for: versioned records committed before their outcomes exist turn "no edge found" from an anecdote into a citable result.

The repo's own experiments have already landed where the literature landed, once ahead of publication, which is evidence that its process works. The lab should spend that credibility on a question the literature leaves open: whether daily cross-sectional trend survives the 2024–2026 regime on a survivorship-corrected Binance perpetual universe after realistic costs. There, either answer is new. On hourly BTC direction or seconds-scale quoting, the answer is already known.
