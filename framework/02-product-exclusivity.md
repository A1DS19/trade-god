## Product Framework: How to Evaluate What a Project Actually Offers

**Core Definition:**

A product is a solution to a significant problem, and its true value—and often its innovation—comes from how effectively it solves that problem, since the bigger the problem, the bigger the market and opportunity.

At its core, product strength is the level of innovation the project has actually built and currently offers and how it creates utility in the real world. In crypto, product = substance.

This Product filter will most likely decrease the amount of coins you are submitting at first. We only want diamonds. With practice, your efficiency will increase and then you will be submitting the same (and then more) as before, but they will all be ELITE quality.

**Quality over Quantity**

This product analysis is broken down into the **3 Keys of a Good Product**:

1. Ease of Use
2. Hair-on-Fire
3. Exclusivity Factor

Is it easy to use, is it a hair-on-fire solution, and does it have the exclusivity factor? If not, move on to another project.

---

## 1. Ease of Use

When you try to use this product, does it actually work smoothly, simply, and effectively?

- Always **test out the product yourself** if it is available.
- Then score it on the **Ease of Use Scale** below.

**Ease of Use Scoring Scale (0–10):**
- (0–3) Does not do what it claims. Complex, broken, or mostly vaporware.
- (4–6) Works, but clunky or not as smooth as claimed.
- (7–10) Does what it claims. Genuinely easy, reliable, and effective in practice.

---

## 2. Hair-on-Fire

A hair-on-fire problem is an urgent, painful issue that customers feel they must solve immediately, making them eager to buy a product that delivers real relief.

Does this product innovate or solve a mass problem in a way that users and/or the entire industry urgently feels they must adopt right now?

- If you don't fully understand what the project is doing, **use ChatGPT to teach you** until you understand it fully.
- Now **research and decide**, is there **really** an **urgent** need for this?
- Look for evidence of **real demand** (users, customers, or industries urgently needing this)

**Hair-On-Fire Scoring Scale (0–10) in product page, but 0-6 in the One Page Report:**
- (0–1) Gimmicky or irrelevant
- (2–4) Interesting, but not exciting
- (5–7) Clearly valuable or innovative — sparks curiosity
- (8–10) Undeniable "hair-on-fire" urgency — feels necessary and immediate

**NOTE:** In the final one-page report, Hair-on-Fire is scored 0–6 and lives inside the Narrative section, not Product.

## 3. Exclusivity Factor

Does this project have a unique "WOW" innovation that changed the game and is exclusive to them?

**Definition of Exclusiveness:** A project has a rare and NOT duplicatable edge — something so unique (first-to-market status, legal win, world class backing, official fork approval) that clones will always be second-best.

- **Example 1 — Zora:** Promoted by Coinbase's founder and chosen as the sole social media technology powering Base — no other social media project has that exclusive positioning.
- **Example 2 — XRP:** Winning the SEC lawsuit gave XRP an exclusive legal advantage no other token can replicate, securing legitimacy that clones can't match.
- **Example 3 — Official Blockchain Approval:** If a project is credited with an Ethereum Foundation–approved fork, like CARV (or similar official recognition), that exclusivity cannot be duplicated by competitors, only used by competitors because they need it.

**Exclusivity Factor Scoring Scale (0–10):**
- (0–1) No exclusivity; generic, easily copied
- (2–4) Some differentiation, but not rare or defensible
- (5–7) Clear unique edge (first-to-market or notable feature), but somewhat deniable
- (8–10) Rare, NOT duplicatable advantage (e.g., Coinbase-level solo integration, legal win like XRP, or official blockchain fork approval) that competitors cannot replicate

## Refined Exclusivity Factor (from One Page Report Framework)

The Exclusivity Factor is the "wow" trait of a project — the one innovation that makes you stop and realize: "This changes everything."

It's not just about being first. It's about offering something so useful, powerful, or disruptive that:
- Nobody else has delivered it yet, or
- Others are trying to duplicate it, but the original project's version is clearly the one that matters.

When a project has that Exclusivity Factor, it feels undeniably undervalued — because the market hasn't fully priced in just how unique and irreplaceable that innovation is.

**Traits of a "Wow-Level Exclusivity Factor":**
- 💡 **Category-Creating Utility:** Solves a problem nobody else solved in such a way that the industry has to adapt to it or they will get left behind.
- 🌍 **Universal Usefulness:** You don't have to stretch the story — it's obviously something people need.
- 🏆 **Market Gravity:** Even competitors validate it by copying the idea.
- 🔥 **Emotional Punch:** When you hear it, you don't just nod — you go "Wow, this changes everything."

**Projects with the Exclusivity Factor (per Skool docs):**
- **Render ($RNDR):** First project to tokenize decentralized GPU compute for creators and AI.
- **World Liberty Finance ($WLFI):** Tied to Trump family / sitting US President — political/global exclusivity.
- **Aerodrome ($AERO):** Liquidity hub of Base chain — became Base's Curve at launch.
- **$CARV:** First to fuse multi-account abstraction + ERC-7231 identity + AI agents + data monetization.
